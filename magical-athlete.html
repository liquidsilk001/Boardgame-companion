Magical Athlete

A dice race of pure chaos. Draft a team of absurd racers — each with a deliberately game-breaking power — then run them four times across the Mild and Wild tracks. When everyone's broken, nobody is.

  • 2–6 players
  • ~30 min
  • CMYK Games
  • Designer: Takashi Ishida
  • Updated by Richard Garfield
  • Ages 6+

Fast setup

Setup is two snake drafts and a small pile of chips — call it 5 minutes with the draft, 2 without. Everything below matches the rulebook's flow.

The one real win

Stack the trophy chips before you draft: put the 4 gold and 4 silver chips in ascending order with the highest value on the bottom. Every race you take the top chip, so the pile automatically pays more each race — and "save your best racer for last" only makes sense once that stack is built.

Part 1 — the one-time prep

Only needed the first time out of the box: pop the racer meeples and match them to their cards. After that, everything lives as one deck of 36 racer cards plus a bag of meeples.

Part 2 — setup lanes

Lane 1Board & chips

One player

Put the racetrack in the middle, Mild Mile side up. Stack the gold and silver trophies highest-value-on-the-bottom. Set the bronze chips in a reachable pile. Hand everyone a die.

Lane 2Lay out the draft

Another player

Shuffle the 36 racer cards and reveal two per player in a row.

Then together — the draft

Everyone rolls a die; highest unique roll drafts first. Snake draft the row: in turn order each player takes one racer, then in reverse order each takes another — now everyone has 2. Shift the starting player one seat to the left, deal a fresh row of two-per-player, and snake draft again, so everyone ends with 4 racers. Grab your four meeples; the rest go back in the box.

Fast teardown

All 36 cards back into one deck, all meeples into the bag, chips into their pouch. Nothing to keep sorted.

How to play — teach script

Teach this one fast and loose — the rules are tiny and the fun is in the chaos. Bold lines read aloud; italics are notes to you.

0 · Buy-in (30 sec)

"It's a dice race. Roll, move that many spaces, first two to the finish line score. The catch: every racer has a completely broken special power. When everyone's broken, nobody is — and the comedy is watching the powers crash into each other. We race four times; most points wins."

Set the tone: embrace the chaos. This is not a game to optimise; it's a game to enjoy going sideways.

1 · How you win

"Most points after four races. You score by finishing first or second in a race — and a few racer powers hand out bonus bronze chips too."

2 · Choosing a racer

"Each race, everyone picks ONE of their four racers and puts its meeple on the Start space. Across the whole game each of your four racers gets used exactly once — so you're really deciding which racer runs on which track, against which opponents."

Reveal simultaneously in turn order. The meta-game is guessing what everyone else is fielding.

3 · Your turn — the whole loop

"On your turn, roll your die and move your racer that many spaces. Then resolve your racer's power and anything on the space you're on. Some powers are mandatory and always fire; some are optional and say 'you may.' Then it's the next player's turn."

That's the entire engine. Everything interesting is printed on the cards — read a power aloud when it first triggers, not before.

4 · When a race ends — teach this clearly

"A race ends the INSTANT the second racer crosses the finish line. Not when everyone finishes — the moment second place is decided, the race is over. First place takes the top gold chip, second place takes the top silver. Everybody else scores nothing from finishing."

This trips people who expect to keep racing for third. Once two are home, stop.

5 · The two tracks

"The board is double-sided. You alternate: race one on the Mild side, race two on the Wild side, then Mild, then Wild. The Wild track adds oddball spaces that trigger extra effects — more chaos on top of the powers."

6 · Between races

"Set up the next race on the other side of the board. The player who scored the FEWEST points last race — the one whose racer finished furthest back, or was knocked out first — becomes the new first player. Ties roll off."

Falling behind buys you first-player next race. It's the game's only catch-up.

7 · The finish

"After the fourth race, add up all your chips. Most points wins; ties are shared. Because the trophy stack pays more each race, that last race is usually where games are decided."

Not applicable here

No teamwork between your own racers in the standard game — your four never share a track, so you're building racers to collide with other people's picks, not with each other. (The 2–3 player variant is the exception: you run two of your own at once.)

First-game recommendations

  • Draft on vibe. Pick the baby, the banana, the egg because they're funny; reading every power aloud kills the pace.
  • Powers key off OTHER racers. The "best" racer depends entirely on who else is on the track — there's no single strong pick.
  • The rulebook says save your best for last, since the final race is worth the most — but "best" is context, so don't over-plan.
  • When two powers tangle and you can't agree, just roll off. That's the official answer. Don't get stuck in the weeds.

Defer these — do not front-load

  • Every racer's exact power
  • Wild-track special spaces
  • Bronze-chip bonus timing
  • Which racer counters which

There are 36 powers; explaining them up front is impossible and pointless. Let each one land when its racer hits the table.

Quick reference

Tap at the start of each race. It also shows which side of the board you're on. Resets when you reload.

Final race — worth the most points. After the fourth race, most points wins (ties shared).

The turn, in three lines

  1. Everyone picks one racer, meeple on Start. Roll, move that many, resolve your power and the space.
  2. The race ends the instant the 2nd racer finishes: 1st takes top gold, 2nd takes top silver.
  3. Fewest points last race goes first next race. Alternate Mild / Wild tracks.

The handful that break games

  1. Race over the moment second place finishes — not when everyone's home.
  2. Only 1st and 2nd score from finishing. Everything else is power-granted bronze chips.
  3. Each racer is used exactly once across the four races.
  4. Trophy chips ascend — the last race pays the most, so the stack has the biggest chips on the bottom.
  5. Worst finisher goes first next race. Ties roll off.
  6. Stuck on a power interaction? Roll off and move on. Official guidance.
  7. Tracks alternate Mild, Wild, Mild, Wild unless you agreed otherwise up front.
