King of Tokyo: Monster Box

Yahtzee-style dice with a monster brawl on top: roll, reroll, then score points, heal, punch, or bank energy to buy powers. Hold Tokyo for points while the whole table shoots at you. First to 20 points — or the last monster standing — is King. The Monster Box adds Evolutions and Costumes.

  • 2–6 players
  • ~30 min
  • IELLO
  • Designer: Richard Garfield
  • Base + Power Up! + Halloween

Fast setup

The Monster Box is the all-in-one edition: the King of Tokyo base game (2nd edition) plus the Power Up! (Evolution cards) and Halloween (Costume cards) expansions, ten monsters, a dice tray and some exclusive cards. Base setup is a couple of minutes; the two expansions add one step each.

The one real win

Keep each monster's eight Evolution cards clipped with its board. They're monster-specific, so a shuffled-together pile means someone hand-sorts 80+ cards by monster before the next game. Bag them per monster and the Power Up step becomes "grab your deck."

Part 1 — the one-time prep

This box mixes three products. Decide before your first game whether you're playing with the expansions or just the base — with new players, teach the base game first and fold in Evolutions and Costumes on game two.

What's in the box (and what isn't)

The Monster Box bundles the base game, Power Up! and Halloween with 10 monsters and a fresh Baby Gigazaur. Monsters released after it (such as Anubis and King Kong) are not included, despite older "all expansions" marketing.

Part 2 — setup lanes

1Pick monsters & set dials

Everyone

Each player takes a monster figure and its board, then sets Health to 10 and Victory Points to 0 on the dials.

2Place Tokyo

One person

Put the Tokyo board in the middle. With 4 players or fewer, use Tokyo City only; with 5–6 players, also use Tokyo Bay. Nobody starts in Tokyo.

3Evolution decks (Power Up!)

Everyone

Take the 8 Evolution cards for your monster, shuffle them, and keep the face-down stack by your board.

4First player

Everyone rolls

Everyone rolls the six black dice; whoever shows the most claws takes the first turn.

Fast teardown

Reset both dials, bag each monster's Evolution deck with its board, and shuffle the Costume cards back into the Power deck.

How to play — teach script

Teach the base loop first, then add the two expansion layers at the end. Bold lines are meant to be read aloud; italics are notes to you, the teacher.

0 · Buy-in (20 sec)

"You're giant monsters fighting over Tokyo. Every turn you roll six dice like Yahtzee — keep what you like, reroll the rest — then do what the dice say: score points, heal, punch each other, or collect energy to buy powers. First to 20 points wins. Or just be the last monster standing."

Frame it as Yahtzee with a fight. That single sentence gets everyone playing.

1 · How you win

"Two ways: reach 20 Victory Points, or knock everyone else out. You do have to survive your own turn to claim a win."

Note the edge case now so it isn't a surprise: a card can push you to 20 and also kill you on the same turn.

2 · Rolling — the Yahtzee part

"Roll all six dice. You may reroll any dice you want up to two more times — three rolls total — keeping and re-keeping dice between rolls however you like. The faces are: 1, 2, 3, a heart, a claw, and an energy bolt."

3 · Resolving the dice

"Now do everything the final dice show, in any order:"

  • Numbers: three of a kind scores that number in points — three 1s = 1, three 2s = 2, three 3s = 3. Each extra matching die is +1 more.
  • Hearts: heal 1 health each — but you can't heal while you're in Tokyo, and never above 10.
  • Claws: deal 1 damage each. If you're outside Tokyo you hit whoever's in Tokyo; if you're in Tokyo you hit everyone outside.
  • Energy: take 1 energy cube each — that's your currency for buying cards.

The claw rule is the heart of the game: one monster in Tokyo versus everyone else. Say it slowly.

4 · Tokyo — the core tension

"If Tokyo is empty and you roll at least one claw, you MUST move in. Being in Tokyo scores points — 1 for entering, and 2 at the start of each of your turns — but you can't heal there, and you take everyone's hits. When you get hurt in Tokyo, you may choose to YIELD: step out, and the monster who hit you moves in and scores 1 for entering."

The whole game breathes here: Tokyo pays in points but bleeds health. Deciding when to hold and when to yield is the game.

5 · Buying Power cards

"After your dice, you can spend energy on the three face-up cards. KEEP cards stay in front of you with ongoing powers; DISCARD cards do a one-time thing and are gone. If you hate all three, pay 2 energy to sweep them and reveal three new ones."

6 · Power Up! — Evolutions

"Each monster has its own deck of Evolution cards. Whenever your dice resolve THREE hearts, secretly draw the top card of your Evolution deck — this happens even in Tokyo, and even if you also used those hearts to heal. You can play an Evolution whenever you want; some are permanent, some are one-time."

Trigger is three hearts, drawn on top of the normal heal — not instead of it. If a deck runs out, extra hearts just heal.

Common variant

Many groups also deal each player one starting Evolution (draw two, keep one) so powers show up earlier. That's an optional variant, not the default rule — agree before you start.

7 · Halloween — Costumes

"Costume cards are shuffled into the normal Power deck and bought the same way, usually cheaply. They give strong powers — but they can be stolen. If another monster rolls three or more claws and damages you, they may pay the costume's energy cost again to rip it right off you and wear it themselves."

Costumes make claws doubly threatening: damage and a mugging. The rest of that attack still happens as normal.

8 · "The game changes now" moments

  • You're forced into Tokyo: you're now everyone's target — plan your escape (a yield) or your point grab.
  • Someone crosses ~16 points: the table should gang up on them, usually by refusing to let them sit safely in Tokyo.
  • A big Costume or Keep card lands: re-read what it changes; several rewrite the dice math.

First-game recommendations

  • Don't cling to Tokyo at low health. Points are worthless if you're eliminated — yield and heal.
  • Reroll toward one plan. Chasing points, health and energy at once usually gets you none of them.
  • Buy a couple of cheap Keep cards early. Small ongoing bonuses compound over a 30-minute game.
  • Watch the leader, not your own dial. King of Tokyo is won by stopping the runaway as much as by racing.

Defer these — do not front-load

  • Specific card combos
  • Evolution timing tricks
  • Costume-steal baiting
  • Energy-engine builds

The base loop is enough for turn one. Evolutions and Costumes explain themselves the first time three hearts or three claws show up.

Quick reference

Roll 1 of 3

Tap for each reroll. You get three rolls total — keep any dice you like between them.

The dice faces

FaceEffect
1 / 2 / 3Three of a kind = that many VP; each extra = +1
HeartHeal 1 (not in Tokyo; max 10)
Claw1 damage — outside hits Tokyo; inside hits everyone outside
EnergyGain 1 energy cube

A turn, in order

  1. In Tokyo? Score +2 VP to start.
  2. Roll 6 dice, up to 3 rolls, keeping dice between rolls.
  3. Resolve: numbers → VP, hearts → heal, claws → damage, energy → cubes.
  4. Tokyo empty + you rolled a claw? Enter (+1 VP). Hurt in Tokyo? You may yield.
  5. Spend energy on Power/Costume cards (sweep 3 for 2 energy).
  6. Three hearts? Draw an Evolution. Pass the dice left.

The handful that break games

  1. You can't heal in Tokyo.
  2. Empty Tokyo + a claw = you must enter.
  3. You only leave Tokyo by yielding after taking damage.
  4. Three of a kind scores the number, not the sum (three 3s = 3, not 9).
  5. Three hearts draws an Evolution — on top of healing.
  6. Three claws can rip a Costume off the monster you hit (pay its cost).
  7. You must survive your turn to win at 20.
