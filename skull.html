Skull

The quintessential bluffing game: three roses, one skull, and a bet you can flip discs face-up without hitting bone. No luck, no cards to draw — the whole game is played in the other players' heads.

  • 3–6 players
  • 15–45 min
  • Lui-même · Space Cowboys
  • Designer: Hervé Marly
  • Guides written for 4–6 players

Fast setup

Skull is the fastest setup on this site — about 20 seconds. There's nothing to bag, sort or scale, so this is deliberately short. The only thing worth getting right is that every player starts with the correct four discs.

The one real win

At teardown, keep each player's coloured set of 4 discs clipped or bagged together. They all look different by colour, so a loose pile means someone re-sorts 16–24 discs by hand next time. Bag them by colour and setup becomes "grab a set."

Part 1 — the one-time prep

There is no folder of punch-out work here. Skull is a handful of discs (or cards, in older printings) and one mat per player. Nothing is player-count dependent.

Part 2 — setup lanes

There's not much to split. Hand each player their mat and their four matching discs, everyone checks their set, and pick a start player. That's the whole setup.

AllTake a set & a mat

Everyone at once

Each player takes one colour's 4 discs (3 roses, 1 skull) and the matching mat, placed in front of them, plain side up.

Any method

Choose a starting player any way you like. After the first round, the start player is always whoever issued the last challenge (win or lose).

Player-count quick reference

Nothing about a player's own kit changes with the count — everyone always has the same 4 discs. The only thing that scales is how many discs can be on the table, which sets the ceiling on a bid.

Fast teardown

Bag or clip each colour's 4 discs together, drop the mats on top, done. There is no sorting to undo.

How to play — teach script

Teach with a set of discs in your hand so you can demo a placement. The bold lines are written to be read aloud; the italics are notes to you, the teacher.

0 · Buy-in (20 sec)

"This is pure bluffing — no luck, no cards to draw, nothing random. Everything happens in your heads and your faces. You each have three roses and one skull. You'll be betting that you can flip a bunch of discs face-up and find only roses. Flip a skull and you fail."

Set expectations: it's short, it's psychological, and the whole game is reading people.

1 · How you win

"First player to win two challenges wins the game. You can also win by being the last person with any discs left — everyone else gets knocked out."

Two wins, not one. Say it twice; people forget and stop at one.

2 · Starting a round

"Every round starts the same way: everyone secretly picks one disc and places it face-down on their mat, all at once."

That first placement is simultaneous and mandatory — nobody bids on the opening beat.

3 · Going around — the choice on your turn

"After that, we go clockwise. On your turn you do ONE of two things: add another disc face-down on top of your stack, or start a challenge. If you've already placed all four of your discs, you can't add another, so you must challenge."

  • Add a disc — place it face-down, slightly offset so everyone can see how many you've played.
  • Challenge — you may do this on your turn even if you could still add a disc.

The tension: more discs down means more of your own roses are locked in and known to you — but it also means you'll owe a bigger bid if you're the one who challenges.

4 · The challenge — the core loop — teach this properly

"A challenge is a bid: 'I can flip THIS MANY discs face-up, from anywhere on the table, without hitting a skull.' Going clockwise, everyone else either raises the bid higher or passes for the rest of the round. Once you pass, you're out until next round. When everyone but one has passed, that last, highest bidder is the Challenger."

"The bid can never be higher than the total number of discs actually on the table this round."

This is the whole game. Bidding is public; passing is permanent for the round.

5 · Revealing

"The Challenger now flips discs face-up, one at a time, counting toward their bid. You MUST start with your own stack, flipping from the top down. After your own are done, you can flip any other player's discs, in any order you like — but always from the top of that player's stack downward."

  • All roses, bid reached → success. You win the challenge: flip your mat to its won side. That's one win.
  • A skull appears → instant fail. Stop flipping immediately.

"Own stack first" is the rule people miss — and it's why baiting works: a player can sit on their own skull hoping you overbid.

6 · What failing costs

"If you fail, the player whose skull you flipped takes one of your discs at random and removes it from the game — you don't get to pick, and you don't get to see which one it was. Lose all four discs and you're eliminated."

Because the removed disc is secret, nobody — including you — knows for sure whether you still have your skull. That uncertainty is a weapon.

7 · "The game changes now" moments

  • You win your first challenge: you're now one win from taking the game. Everyone will target your bids.
  • You lose a disc to a failed challenge: you have fewer discs to bluff with — and might have lost your skull without knowing.
  • You're down to one disc: one more failed challenge and you're out.
Not applicable here

No hidden setup, no variable powers, no luck. Every player has the identical kit. There is nothing to draft, draw, or randomise — only what you choose to place and what you choose to say.

First-game recommendations

  • Bid low and safe early. Your first couple of wins can come from small, certain bids on your own two roses.
  • Watch who adds discs vs. who challenges. A player piling on discs may be building a safe bid — or hiding a skull.
  • Remember you flip your own stack first. Don't challenge past your own hidden skull.
  • Talk. Banter, misdirection and table talk are the game. Silent Skull is a worse game.

Defer these — do not front-load

All of this emerges after one round. Teach the loop, play a hand, and the strategy explains itself.

Quick reference

Discs on the table: 0

Tap as discs go down. The number shown is the highest legal bid anyone can make this round. Resets when you reload.

The round, in five lines

  1. Everyone places one disc face-down at the same time.
  2. Clockwise, each player either adds a disc face-down or issues a challenge. Out of discs? You must challenge.
  3. A challenge is a bid (≤ total discs on the table). Others raise or pass; passing is out for the round.
  4. The last, highest bidder is the Challenger: flip own stack first (top-down), then any stacks top-down, until you reach the bid in roses.
  5. All roses = win (flip mat; two wins take the game). A skull = fail (lose a disc at random).

The handful that break games

  1. First to TWO wins — not one. Or be the last player with discs.
  2. The Challenger always reveals their own discs first. Your own hidden skull fails you instantly.
  3. A bid can never exceed the total discs on the table.
  4. Discs flip top-down within a stack. You choose the stack, never the individual disc.
  5. A failed challenge costs one disc at random, chosen and hidden by the skull's owner. You may lose your skull without knowing.
  6. Passing during bidding is permanent for that round.
  7. The Challenger starts the next round, win or lose.
