Long Shot: The Dice Game

A horse-racing roll-and-write where you're the gambler, not the jockey. Buy horses, place bets, rig the field in small ways — and when the third horse crosses the line, the richest player wins. Play it safe on the favourites, or risk it big on a long shot.

  • 1–8 players
  • ~25 min
  • Perplext
  • Designer: Chris Handy
  • Includes the solo mode

Fast setup

Long Shot: The Dice Game is a roll-and-write, so "setup" is mostly handing out dry-erase boards and lining up eight horses. Two minutes, tops. The only thing to get right is the order of the horses and everyone's starting money.

The one real win

Give every player a fresh cloth or paper towel along with the marker. The boards are dry-erase and smudge easily mid-race, and a half-erased bet is an argument waiting to happen. Also decide up front who is banker/rules-keeper — one person to settle "can I still bet on that horse?" questions.

Part 1 — the one-time prep

The horses come in different sets (an apple-core set, a gate set, and the mini-expansion sets in this box). You only ever use one card per numbered horse. For a first game, pick a single set and keep the mixing for later.

Part 2 — setup lanes

Everything happens at once — there's nothing to build in sequence. One person lays out horses while everyone else sorts their board.

1Line up the field

One person

Place the eight horse cards in a row, #1 through #8, near the shared reference. Set the horses at the start of the track.

2Boards & markers

Everyone

Take a player board and a dry-erase marker. Write $12 in your cash total.

3Starting card

Everyone

Reveal your dealt starting card and mark the free concession coins and free bets it grants onto your board, then set it aside.

4Pick a start player

Any method

Choose a first player however you like. The rulebook jokes it's whoever has the biggest Seabiscuit tattoo — any tie-breaker works.

Player-count quick reference

Because every player takes an action on every roll (not just the active roller), higher counts add less downtime than you'd expect — but 7–8 players still means a lot of simultaneous marking. The game supports a full solo mode too.

Wipe every board clean before the marker sets, square up the horse cards by set, done.

How to play — teach script

Teach this with a board in front of you so you can point at the concession grid and bet squares. Bold lines are meant to be read aloud; italics are notes to you, the teacher.

0 · Buy-in (30 sec)

"You are gamblers at the track, not jockeys. Eight horses race; you'll buy horses, place bets, and rig the race in small ways. When the third horse crosses the line the race ends and the richest player wins — it doesn't matter who owns the winning horse, only who made the most money."

The key reframe: you win with money, from many sources, not by "your" horse winning.

1 · The field

"Horses are numbered by how likely they are to win. #1 is the favourite on the inside — short run, small payout. #8 is the long shot on the outside — furthest to go, biggest payout if it lands."

Point at the odds on each card. Big odds on #7/#8 are the "risk it big" bets the title is named for.

2 · A turn — only one person rolls

"On your turn you roll two dice: the 8-sided die picks WHICH horse moves, the 6-sided die says HOW FAR. Move that horse. Then check its card — it has little X's pointing at other horses, and those horses each move one space too. Always move horses in number order, lowest to highest."

"Here's the twist: only the active player rolls, but EVERYONE — including you — then takes one action based on that roll."

This is the engine that keeps everyone engaged between their own rolls. Teach it slowly; the "everyone acts" beat is the one people miss.

3 · Your action — pick exactly one

"Everyone picks one of these, always in relation to the horse number that was just rolled. Then the dice pass on."

  • Concession — cross out a coin in your grid matching the rolled horse's number. Complete a full row or column and you claim a prize (money, free bets, a free helmet/jersey/horse, or nudging horses forward/back).
  • Bet — put up to $3 on the horse that just moved (write it in that horse's square; pay from cash). You can raise an existing bet on a later turn.
  • Buy the horse — pay its cost, take the card. You gain its special power and an end-race bonus if it places. Can't buy a finished horse or one already owned.
  • Buy the jersey — mark the jersey, then link that horse to drag another along whenever it moves (boosting its odds).
  • Buy the helmet — mark the helmet; it lets you keep betting on that horse even after it crosses the no-bet line.

The rolled number constrains almost everything — which is why the horseshoe wilds (next) are precious.

4 · Horseshoe wilds & mulligans

"Crossing off a horseshoe lets you take your action on ANY horse, ignoring the number that was rolled. And if a roll gives you nothing useful, you can erase a horseshoe as a mulligan — but that's your whole action for the turn."

5 · The no-bet line & the finish

"There's a red 'no-bet' line late on the track. Once a horse crosses it, nobody can start or raise bets on it — unless you own that horse's helmet. When the THIRD horse crosses the finish, the race is over; no more horses can finish."

The rule everyone forgets

A horse can only win by moving from a dice roll or from being dragged by a jersey link. You can never push a horse across the finish using a concession "move ahead" prize — it stops just short of the line.

6 · Scoring — tally when the third horse is home

  1. Owned winners: $35 / $25 / $15 for owning the 1st / 2nd / 3rd horse (stacks if you own several).
  2. Helmet + jersey sets: $5 for each horse where you own both.
  3. Bets: for each placing horse you bet on, multiply your bet by that horse's payout odds for where it finished.
  4. Bet-backs: for a horse that was past the no-bet line at the end, you simply get your bet back.
  5. Cash on hand: add whatever money you have left.

Highest total wins. Ties go to whoever owns the highest-placing horse.

7 · "The game changes now" moments

  • A horse crosses the no-bet line: the window to bet on it just closed — grab it now or own its helmet.
  • A horse reaches the winner's circle: one of the three finishing slots is gone; the race is closer to ending.
  • You complete a concession row/column: stop and take the prize before continuing — it's easy to overlook.

First-game recommendations

  • Bet small and often on the favourites. Low odds, but they finish most races.
  • Own at least one horse. The placing bonus plus its power usually pays for itself.
  • Don't hoard horseshoes. A well-timed wild that lets you bet on the leader is worth more than saving it.
  • Watch the third-place race. Everything ends the instant that third horse is home.

Defer these — do not front-load

  • Optimal concession pathing
  • Jersey-link combos
  • Individual horse powers
  • Long-shot payout math

Play one race and the board teaches itself. Teach the roll, the one action, and the finish; the rest lands after a lap.

Quick reference

Finishers: 0 of 3

Tap each time a horse crosses the finish line. At the third, the race ends — stop and tally.

A turn, in four lines

  1. Active player rolls the horse die (1–8) and the move die (1–6).
  2. Move that horse; then each horse it links to (X's / jerseys) moves +1. Resolve low number → high.
  3. Everyone takes one action tied to the rolled horse: Concession · Bet (≤$3) · Buy horse · Buy jersey · Buy helmet. (Horseshoe = any horse, or mulligan.)
  4. Pass the dice clockwise. Third horse home = race ends.

The handful that break games

  1. Only the active player rolls — but everyone acts off that roll.
  2. One action per player per roll. Always tied to the rolled horse (or a horseshoe).
  3. Concession nudges can't finish a horse. Only dice or jersey-link movement can cross the line.
  4. No new/raised bets past the red line unless you own that horse's helmet.
  5. Race ends the instant the third horse finishes.
  6. Richest player wins — not the horse's owner.
