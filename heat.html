Heat: Pedal to the Metal

A 1960s Grand Prix in a box. Your deck is your engine, the pack stays bunched all race, and the whole game turns on one idea: Heat is a loan, not a cost.

Be realistic about the win

Heat's setup is already short. The parallel lanes below save maybe two minutes on a base race. Part 1 is where the real time is, and it pays off hardest in Championship play, where four different decks share the same card back and one bad teardown costs you fifteen minutes next session.

Part 1 — the one-time bagging pass

The goal: setup becomes grab bag → deal → shuffle, never sort.

The never-mix pairs

Heat has three, and all three are genuinely easy to get wrong.

If your group always plays Garage

Build the colour kits without the Starting Upgrades and keep those in the never-mix bag instead. Pick one convention and stay consistent — a kit that sometimes contains them is worse than either option.

Part 2 — parallel setup, 5 lanes

The base rulebook's setup is a bulleted list rather than numbered steps, so the lanes below reference them as S1–S7 in printed order: S1 board · S2 colour components · S3 check board for Heat/Stress · S4 build deck, engine and gear pawn · S5 Stress reserve · S6 starting grid · S7 draw 7.

Lane 1Board

Your fastest reader

S1. Place the board on the correct track side. Read out the lap count, and read out the Heat and Stress numbers printed on it — the whole table needs these.

Lane 2Reserves

S3, S5. Count the printed Heat per car into five piles. Put the remaining Heat and all remaining Stress face up beside the board as the reserve.

Lane 3Kits

S2. Hand out the five colour bags. Confirm out loud whether Starting Upgrades are in or out tonight.

Lane 4Modulesskip for a base race

Shuffle the Upgrade deck (Regular only for early Garage games). If playing Weather: draw 1 Weather token onto the Billboard, then draw one Road Conditions token per corner on the track. This must happen before the draft, so people can build cars for the conditions.

Lane 5Grid

S6. Once cars exist, randomly fill the starting grid one car at a time, ascending spots.

Lane ordering

Lane 5 is the only real dependency — it can't start until Lane 3 has handed out cars. Lane 4 gets its own person because the Road Conditions draw is conditional (a token showing the sector symbol goes on the sector tent, one without it goes on the corner tent); it's the one fiddly step in the whole setup. And because Weather changes how many Heat and Stress you take, Lane 4 must finish before Lane 2 deals the engines — in practice, run Lane 4 first and have Lane 2 wait ten seconds.

Then together — about 60 seconds
  1. Everyone shuffles their own deck — 12 Speed + 3 Stress + 3 Upgrades — and places it on the mat.
  2. Heat face up on the Engine spot. Gear pawn to 1st.
  3. Garage draft, if playing. Genuinely sequential, three rounds — see below.
  4. Everyone draws 7. Lights out.

The Garage draft at 5 players

Three rounds. Each round: lay out a market of 8 cards (one per player, +3). Pick one at a time, discard the leftovers, lay out a fresh 8.

  • Round 1 — last car on the grid picks first, working forwards.
  • Round 2 — pole car picks first, working backwards.
  • Round 3 — last car picks first again.

Shuffle your three into your draw deck. This is the last step before drawing 7 — don't let anyone draw their hand early or they'll draw before their upgrades are in.

Not verified

The exact printed Heat / Stress / lap values for each of the four tracks could not be confirmed from the official PDFs — that data lives on the boards themselves. It's typically 6 Heat and 3 Stress, but read it off the board each time you change track. Don't assume 6/3 carries over.

Fast teardown — two minutes, non-negotiable

  1. Colour kits first, while cards are still sorted by colour on the table — 12 Speed + 3 Starting Upgrades + mat + car + pawn back into the colour bag.
  2. Heat and Stress counted back into their own two bags. Do not tip them into one pile "to sort later."
  3. Upgrades split back into Regular and Advanced before they go anywhere. This is the step people skip and the one that costs the most next time.
  4. Championship decks re-banded separately — they share a back and you cannot fix this by looking at the pile.
  5. Tokens back to their two bags.

How to play — teach script

About 9 minutes for the base race, plus 90 seconds if you're adding the Garage draft. Teach after setup, board visible. Bold lines are for reading aloud.

An honest note on length

Heat does not need a 15-minute teach. Padding it out only front-loads things the board teaches better. There's no predecessor to compare against and no hidden information, so both of those sections are simply cut.

0 · Buy-in (30 sec)

"This is a 1960s Grand Prix race. You're a driver, and your deck of cards is your engine. Everybody stays bunched together the whole race — this is not a game where someone drives off into the distance. It's won by two or three spaces on the last corner, and being at the back is genuinely fine, because the back of the pack gets help."

The failure mode in Heat's teach is people thinking it's a euro about deck optimisation. It's a pack game. Set the expectation that they'll be surrounded by cars for an hour and that the lead changes constantly, or the first twenty minutes feel like nothing is happening.

1 · How you win

"First car across the finish line after two laps wins. That's it. No points, no scoring."

Say the lap count off the board. For a first race, use the USA track and consider one lap instead of two — the rulebook itself suggests this.

2 · The round structure

"Every round, all of us at the same time pick a gear and play that many cards face down. Then we resolve one at a time — the car at the front of the pack goes first, then the next, back to last place. When your turn comes you flip your cards, move, and react. Then you refill to seven cards."

Two lines. The nine numbered steps on the player mat are a reference, not a teach. Walk the first round live instead.

3 · Your turn — gears are your action economy

"Your gear is how many cards you play this round. First gear, one card. Fourth gear, four cards. Each round you may shift one gear up or down for free. Shifting two gears costs you one Heat."

"Your hand is seven cards. You refill to seven at the end of every turn. Cards are worth 1 to 4."

Then teach only these four things you can do:

  • Play your cards — add the values, move exactly that many spaces.
  • Cool down — in 1st gear you may return 3 Heat cards from your hand to your engine; in 2nd gear, 1.
  • Boost — once per turn, pay 1 Heat and flip off the top of your deck until a Speed card comes up. Add it.
  • Slipstream — if you finish next to or behind another car, move 2 more spaces, free.

"Anything else you see on a card, we'll read when it turns up."

4 · The core loop — Heat (teach this one properly)

Spend your budget here. Point at the engine spot on the mat.

"You have six Heat cards sitting in your engine. Three things move a Heat card out of the engine and into your discard pile: taking a corner faster than its speed limit, boosting, and skipping a gear."

"That Heat card is not gone. Your discard pile becomes your deck. So in a few rounds, that Heat card is in your hand — and Heat cards cannot be played and cannot be discarded. It just sits there, making your hand smaller."

"The only way out is to slow down. Drop to first or second gear and cool down — that's the Heat card going back into the engine, ready to be spent again."

"So: Heat is a loan, not a cost. You can always afford it right now. You pay it back three rounds later, at a moment the game chooses, not you."

This is the one rule that must land precisely. Every other rule in Heat can be looked up mid-race. If the table understands the loan, they understand the game.

5 · What hurts you

"If you go through a corner over the speed limit, you pay Heat equal to the difference. If you can't pay it, you spin out: you pay everything you have, your car goes back before the corner, you take Stress cards into your hand, and you drop to first gear."

"Stress cards are the other clutter. You start with three shuffled into your deck. When you play one, you don't know its value — you flip off your deck until a Speed card shows up, and that's what it's worth. Sometimes 1, sometimes 4."

"There's a rare, ugly one worth knowing about: if your hand fills with so much Heat that you can't fill your gear with playable cards, your car doesn't move at all that round. It won't happen if you cool down. It will happen if you never do."

The cluttered hand is the punishment that lands rounds after the mistake. Announce it, don't detail the cure.

6 · The clock

"There's no timer. The laps are the clock. Watch the numbers printed on the straights — they tell you how many spaces to the next corner, so you can work out whether the hand you're holding gets you there cleanly."

7 · The catch-up layer (land this last)

"With five of us, the last two cars to move each round get Adrenaline: one extra space of movement, and one extra cooldown. Every round. Being behind is a resource here. Do not panic when you're last — you get paid for it."

8 · "The game changes now" moments

Name these; don't resolve them yet.

  • The first shuffle (usually round five to seven). Your discard pile — now full of the Heat you spent — becomes your deck. The game gets noticeably harder here and people are always surprised.
  • The first spin-out. It looks catastrophic and mostly isn't.
  • The final lap. No slipstreaming across the finish line, and once you're past the line you ignore corner speed limits entirely — just move as far as you can.

First-game recommendations

The three mantras. Repeat these whenever someone hesitates:

  1. "Heat is a loan, not a cost."
  2. "Count to the corner." The spaces-to-next-corner numbers are printed on the track and nobody looks at them for the first three rounds.
  3. "Slipstream doesn't count, boost does" — for the corner check. This is the single most misplayed rule in the game.
  • Let the components teach. Don't explain upgrade symbols, weather tokens or the Hall of Fame. Read them off the card the moment they matter.
  • Appoint an Adrenaline caller. It's the most-forgotten step in every group. Give one player the job of saying "last two — you have Adrenaline" at the top of each round.
  • The real teaching moment is the first corner. When the leading car reaches corner one, stop and walk the whole table through the check together: total the speed, compare to the limit, pay the difference. Do it once, slowly, out loud. You will not need to do it again.

Add-on: teaching the Garage draft (90 seconds, race two)

Only teach this once they've finished a base race.

"Instead of the three starting upgrade cards everyone had last game, we each build our own car by drafting three upgrades before the race."

"We lay out eight cards — one per player plus three. We pick one at a time, and the car in last place on the grid picks first. Then we clear the leftovers and lay out eight new ones, and this time the pole car picks first. Third round, back to last-picks-first. Shuffle your three into your deck."

"Two things to know when you play them. If a symbol has the mandatory icon, you must do it — that's the price of the card. Everything else is optional and you can do it in any order. And resolve symbols first, then pick the speed value on any card that offers you a choice."

For the first Garage race, use Regular upgrades only — the Advanced ones stack multiple effects and slow the table down. If the draft feels like too much on the night, the rulebook's own shortcut is to deal three random Regular upgrades to each player and start.

Quick reference

Corner check

Total speed includes played cards, Stress values and Boost. It does not include Slipstream.

Enter your speed and the corner's limit.

The round — nine steps

Steps 1, 2, 3 and 9 happen every round. Steps 4–8 are situational.

  1. Shift gears — one gear free, two gears costs 1 Heat, paid immediately. Everyone at once.
  2. Play cards — face down, exactly as many as your gear. Everyone at once.
  3. Reveal & move — add values, move exactly that many spaces. Resolve Stress cards now. Front car to back car from here.
  4. Adrenaline — last car, or last two with 5+ cars: +1 space and +1 Cooldown.
  5. React — activate Cooldown and Boost symbols in any order you like.
  6. Slipstream — ended next to or behind another car? Move 2 more, optional.
  7. Check corner — crossed a Corner Line? Pay Heat equal to speed over the limit.
  8. Discard — optional. Never Heat, never Stress.
  9. Replenish hand — play area to discard, draw back up to 7.

The five that break races

  1. Boost counts for the corner check. Slipstream does not. The single most misplayed rule in the game.
  2. Cooldown is 3 in 1st gear and 1 in 2nd — and Adrenaline adds one more.
  3. Cross several corners in one turn and you pay for each separately, starting with the first Corner Line you crossed.
  4. You can never discard Heat or Stress — including Upgrade cards that are Heat.
  5. Adrenaline goes by cars that started the race, not cars still running. It doesn't shift as people finish.

Spin out — exactly what happens

  1. Pay all the Heat you have.
  2. Move your car back to the first available space before the corner that caused it.
  3. Take 1 Stress into hand if you were in 1st/2nd gear, 2 Stress if in 3rd/4th.
  4. Move your gear pawn to 1st.

Final lap

  • No slipstreaming once you've crossed the finish line — and you can't slipstream across it either.
  • Ignore corner speed limits after the finish line. Just move as far as you can.
  • Two cars finish in the same round? Furthest ahead wins; side by side, the spot closest to the Race Line is ahead.
  • Finished cars come off the track at end of round and go to the Hall of Fame. Keep racing until everyone's home.
