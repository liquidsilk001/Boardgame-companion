Nemesis: Lockdown

Semi-cooperative survival horror in a Mars facility that's already lost. Noise brings monsters, the lights are going out on a timer, and only some of you are getting home.

  • 1–5 players
  • 90–180 min
  • Awaken Realms
  • Guides written for 5 players

Fast setup

Two things make Lockdown slow: re-sorting components every game, and one person doing 21 sequential steps while everyone watches. This kills both. Do Part 1 once; do Part 2 every game.

Target: under 10 minutes to table, down from the usual 25–30.

Part 1 — the one-time bagging pass

The rulebook has you fish loose tokens out of a shared pool every game. Bag by type a single time instead, and setup becomes grab bag → dump → shuffle, never sort. Small ziplocks are enough; a foamcore or plastic insert (Folded Space and Feldherr both make a Lockdown tray) does the same job and holds the sort.

If you always play 5

Also pre-trim both Objective decks once — remove cards showing a player count above 5 — and band them. If your count varies, skip this and trim per game in Lane 5.

Part 2 — parallel setup (~8 min with 5 players)

One lane per player, all five running at the same time. Lanes are ordered so nothing waits on anything else — the only dependency is that Lane 1 places the fixed rooms early, which it does first.

Lane 1Maplongest lane — give your fastest sorter

Steps 1–6. Board out, basic side (red arrow, top-left). Place the 3 fixed rooms: Isolation Room no-action side up, Repository, Backup Power Supply. Shuffle "II" tiles face-down and place one on each II slot. Shuffle "I" tiles face-down and place one on each I slot. Place one Underground Exploration token face-down on every unrevealed tile. Place the Elevator, Main Gate and Rover.

Lane 2Power & clock

Steps 7, 8, 13, 14. Shuffle Power tokens: 3 into the Section slots + 1 on the Elevator; tokens 1 & 2 blue side up, 3 & 4 red side up. Shuffle CSS tokens, one face-down on each CSS slot of the Time track (one token back to the box). Set Alert, Autodestruction and Venting tokens beside the board. Place the Time token red-side up on the 15th (green) slot.

Lane 3Intruders & lab

Steps 9, 10. Lab board out: 5 Eggs in the Nest; 3 random Weakness cards — reveal the leftmost, others face-down. Build the Intruder bag: base-10 bag + 1 Adult per player from reserve. Set the reserve tokens and Carcasses beside the board.

Lane 4Decks

Step 11. Shuffle and set out: 3 Item decks, Event, Attack, Contamination, Serious Wound. Shuffle the Computer Action deck into its slot and reveal the top card — if it's a "Lock-down," reveal the next and shuffle Lock-down back in. Set the Crafted Item deck by the Item decks and the Scanner by the Contamination deck.

Lane 5Supply & kits

Steps 12, 15–18. Set the marker pool and dice beside the board (Fire, Malfunction, Noise, Ammo/Injury, Status, Doors, Corpses, 2 Combat + 2 Noise + 2 Advantage dice). Deal 1 Help card + matching Inventory to each player (Help cards numbered 1–5). Trim and shuffle both Objective decks, deal each player 1 Corporate + 1 Personal (kept secret). Shuffle Contingency tokens: 1 to each player (secret), 1 face-down on the Active Contingency slot, the rest to the Neutral Pool by the Alert Room.

Then together — about 90 seconds
  1. Character draft (step 19). Shuffle the 6 Character Draft cards. Player 1 flips 2, keeps 1, shuffles the other back; then Player 2, and so on. This one must be sequential.
  2. Grab your kit (step 20). Everyone takes their character's pre-built kit at once: board, shuffled Action deck to the left, Starting Item in a hand slot with ammo markers equal to its capacity, Quest Item set inactive beside the board, Knowledge token inactive-side up on the Lab board. Place your miniature — Nemesis Survivors (Survivor, Lab Rat) start in the Isolation Room; Facility Workers start in the Repository. Knowledge token starts on slot 1 for Facility Workers, slot 3 for everyone else.
  3. First Player token to Player 1 (step 21). Done.

Fast teardown

Setup only stays fast if teardown respects the bags. Reverse Part 1: decks back to their bands, tokens back to their type-bags, the six player kits reassembled into their character bags, exploration tokens kept Underground separate from Surface, and the Intruder base-10 counted back into its bag (pull the per-player Adults back to reserve). Two minutes of discipline here is what buys the sub-10-minute setup next time.

How to play — teach script

A 12–15 minute table teach for 5 players. Read the bold lines aloud or paraphrase; italics are notes for you. The golden rule of teaching heavy games: teach the skeleton, let the organs reveal themselves in play.

0 · Before you start talking

Make sure everyone can see the board with its rooms, the Intruder bag, the Time track (token on the 15th/green slot), and the deck row. Each player should already have their character board, Action deck, starting item, two secret Objectives, and a Contingency token.

"This is a semi-co-op survival horror game. We're employees trapped in a Mars facility that's been overrun. We help each other because the monsters will kill us — but each of you has a secret goal, and only some of us will make it out. You can win alone, win together, or all die. Expect your best plan to fall apart. That's the game working, not the game breaking."

This buy-in matters. People need to know up front it's not pure co-op and that death and failure are normal and fun.

1 · How you win (give them the target first)

"To win, you need TWO things. One: complete your secret objective. Two: survive — which means getting out one of three ways: lock yourself in the Isolation Room, launch out in a Cargo Pod, or reach the Bunker. If you die, you can't win, no matter what your objective was."

Don't explain the escape routes in detail yet — just plant that there are three exits and survival is mandatory. Details come when someone heads for one.

2 · The round — two phases

"Each round has two phases. First the Player Phase — we take our turns. Then the Event Phase — the facility fights back: monsters attack and move, the clock ticks, and a new monster may wake up. Then repeat."

That's all they need on structure. You'll walk the Event Phase live the first time it happens.

3 · Your turn = 2 actions

"At the start of the round you draw up to 5 cards. On your turn you take TWO actions, then it passes left. Here's the twist that makes this game tick: your cards ARE your actions AND your fuel. Most actions cost cards — you pay by discarding cards from your hand. So your hand is your clock. Spend it fast and you're helpless when a monster shows up."

Then teach the core actions only:

  • Move — go to a neighbouring room. If it's unexplored, flip the tile and resolve its token. If you enter an empty room, roll the Noise die.
  • Careful Move — same, but instead of rolling for noise you choose a corridor to put a noise marker in. Safer, more predictable.
  • Search — dig for items in the room, using the room's item counter.
  • Shoot / Melee — attack a monster in your room.

"There are more actions — on your cards, your items, and printed on rooms — but you'll read those as they come up. Move, search and fight are 90% of it."

Do NOT enumerate every action card. Let them read their own deck.

4 · Noise is the monster spawner (the heart of the game)

"When you enter an empty room, you roll the Noise die. On a 1–4, you put a noise marker in the matching corridor. Here's the danger: a corridor can only hold ONE noise marker. If you'd put noise where there's already noise — that's an Encounter. You reach into the bag and pull out whatever's been lurking."

"Some results say 'Danger' — that pulls nearby monsters toward you. 'Silence' means you got lucky, nothing happens. So: moving is loud, and noise you leave behind is a trap for the next person who makes more of it. Quiet and careful beats fast and reckless."

Emphasise the emotional takeaway: noise accumulates and bites you. This is what makes the game tense. Everything else is detail.

"On an Encounter you draw a monster token, put that monster in your room, then compare the number on the token to how many cards are in your hand. Fewer cards than the number = it gets a free surprise attack. So an empty hand in a dark corner is how people die."

5 · Fighting (and why you usually shouldn't)

"You can Shoot — spend ammo, roll the combat die — or Melee with your bare hands. But melee is desperate: you draw a Contamination card just for trying, and if you miss you take a Serious Wound. And here's the horror-movie part: you never know how much health a monster has. You hit it, then draw a card to see if that was enough. It often isn't."

"The lesson: this isn't a game about killing monsters. It's about avoiding them and getting your job done. Fight when cornered, not for sport."

Set this expectation hard or your group will waste ammo and die trying to clear rooms.

6 · The two ways the facility kills you

"Two damage tracks. Wounds: light wounds pile up, three light equals one serious, three serious plus any more and you're dead. Straightforward."

"Contamination is the scary one. Some attacks give you Contamination cards. They clog your hand — you can't spend them — and each one hides whether you're INFECTED. You use the red Scanner to check. Get infected and a parasite goes on your board; get infected again while it's there and you're dead. Even if you survive to the end, there's a final contamination check — if you never cleaned yourself up, you can lose at the finish line. There are rooms and items that cure it."

Don't teach every cure method. Just: contamination is a slow poison, the Scanner reveals it, and there are ways to purge it. They'll find the Decon Room and Surgery when they need them.

7 · What Lockdown adds — Power & Darkness

"The facility is split into three Sections, each with its own power, and power is draining as the clock ticks. A powered section is 'safe-ish': you shoot with a better die, you can use Computers, and it's lit. A section with NO power is Darkness — and monsters are deadlier in the dark: those monster tokens have a second, higher number that gets used when you're in Darkness. There's a Backup Power room and a generator to fight the blackout. So there's a constant tug-of-war between the lights going out and you keeping them on."

If someone's eyes glaze, cut it to: "Powered = better, Dark = scarier, and the lights are going out over time." Teach the Computer and generator specifics when a player stands in one of those rooms.

8 · The clock and the exits (keep it brief)

"The Time token marches right every Event Phase. When it runs out, the corporation purges the facility — anyone still inside dies. So you're on a timer to finish your objective AND get to an exit. The three exits again: the Cargo Pods launch on certain marked turns, the Bunker needs the main gate open and a way across the surface, and the Isolation Room seals you in but only opens partway through the game."

That's enough. The exact requirements are printed and you'll cover them the first time someone makes a break for it.

9 · The secret layer (save this for last so it lands)

"You each have TWO objective cards right now — a Corporate one and a Personal one. The first time ANY of us meets a monster, everyone secretly picks one and burns the other. From then on, that hidden goal is what you're actually playing for — and some objectives put you against the rest of us."

"You also each hold a secret Contingency token. At the very end, one hidden 'corporate code' gets revealed and it can execute some survivors. During the game you can trade, hint at, and lie about this info to figure out which code is live and protect yourself. That's the paranoia engine. You don't have to lean into it hard your first game — but know that not everyone at this table is your friend."

For a first game, tell them it's fine to play mostly cooperatively and just get a feel for survival. The knives can come out next time.

10 · Four "the game changes" moments

Tell them these exist so nobody's blindsided; resolve them when they happen.

  1. First monster encounter → everyone locks in one objective, discards the other.
  2. First character death → the Main Gate opens, unlocking a path to the Bunker.
  3. Isolation Room opens partway through the timer.
  4. Autodestruction goes red → if someone starts the self-destruct and it can't be stopped, the clock to detonation is on.

First-game recommendations

  • Play it loose and survival-focused. Let people learn the noise / combat / contamination loop before anyone tries hard to sabotage a rival.
  • Three mantras to repeat whenever someone forgets: noise is the enemy, don't melee unless desperate, watch your hand size near monsters.
  • Let the room sheets and cards do the talking. When a player enters the Laboratory, Decon Room or Power Generator, read that room's action then — not up front.
  • Appoint a clock watcher — one player who reminds the table each Event Phase to advance the Time token and check power. It's the most-forgotten step.
  • Expect the first Event Phase to be your real teaching moment. Slow-walk it together the first time, then it's muscle memory. There's a step-by-step caller in the Quick ref tab.

Defer these — do not front-load

  • Technical Corridors / vents
  • Every exploration-token symbol
  • Slime
  • Malfunctions & Fire spread
  • Door states
  • Knowledge & Weaknesses
  • Crafting
  • The Nest & Eggs
  • Alert Procedure
  • CSS pod exact timing
  • Enviro-Suit / Rover
  • The full Computer action list
  • Retreat rules

Each is one sentence when it's relevant on the board — a paragraph of confusion if you say it now.

Quick reference

Event Phase caller

The most-forgotten sequence in the game. Hand this to your clock watcher and tap through it.

Step 1 of 7

Launch CSS Pods

Is there a CSS token under the Time token? Reveal it and check which pods launch this round.

Player Phase

  1. Draw Action cards — all players draw up to a hand of 5. Empty deck? Shuffle your discard pile first.
  2. Pass the First Player token one seat left — every round except the first.
  3. Player turns — starting with the first player, clockwise, each player takes a turn of 2 actions. Continue until all have passed. Passing counts as a turn; once you pass you're done for the round.

The six that break games

  1. You do NOT roll for Noise when you enter a room that already has a Character or an Intruder in it. Only empty rooms.
  2. Noise where there is already Noise = an Encounter, not a second marker. A corridor holds one marker, ever.
  3. Surprise attacks compare the token number to your HAND SIZE — Contamination cards count. An empty hand in the dark is how people die.
  4. In Darkness you use the higher red number on the Night Stalker token, not the lower blue one.
  5. Leaving a room with an Intruder is an Escape — the Intruder attacks you first, once per Intruder in the room.
  6. Surviving isn't winning. Contingency check, then contamination check, then objective check — you can die at the very last step.

The three exits

ExitWhat it needs
Isolation RoomTime token has reached a white (number 8) slot — or the Alert Procedure has been activated. Multiple characters can each lock into their own chamber.
CSS Cargo PodBe in a Cargo Sending System room with the Time token on a slot marked with a CSS token. One character per pod. You launch during the Event Phase.
BunkerBe in the Exit Room (002), Main Gate open, no Intruder in the room, and either the Rover present or you carry an Enviro-Suit.

Wounds & death, exactly

  • 1st light wound → marker on the upper space. 2nd → move it to the lower space.
  • 3rd light wound → discard the marker and take a Serious Wound card.
  • At 3 Serious Wounds, any further wound of any kind kills you.
  • Dressing a Serious Wound flips it and cancels its effect, but it still counts toward the limit of 3.
  • Scan an INFECTED card → Larva on your board. Scan another while a Larva is there → you die, and a Creeper appears where you fell.
