Forest Shuffle

Grow a forest one tree at a time, and try to be finished before winter arrives. A low-conflict, family-friendly card game with one genuinely sharp system underneath.

  • 2–5 players
  • 40–60 min
  • Lookout Games
  • Designer: Kosch
  • Guides written for 5 players

Fast setup

Forest Shuffle's setup is already short — roughly 5–7 minutes the first time, under 3 once your group has done it twice. There isn't much to fast-forward, so this is deliberately brief. Step numbers match the official English rulebook.

The one real win

Isolate the 3 Winter cards at teardown, not at setup. The rules have you dig those three cards back out of the full deck every single session. Paperclip them together the moment you box up and next session's step 3 is already done.

Part 1 — the one-time prep

There is no folder full of bagging work here. Forest Shuffle has no map sides, no factions, no per-player decks — just one shuffled 180-card deck and five identical Cave cards.

Part 2 — setup lanes

Deck-building steps are naturally sequential — you can't divide a deck before you've shuffled it — so a full lane-per-player split doesn't apply here. But two things can run side by side.

Lane 1Board & reference

Whoever's closest to the box

Step 1. Place the Clearing board centrally, then fan the 14 reference cards out beside it where everyone can reach them.

Lane 2Deck build

One or two other players

Steps 3–6. Set aside the 3 Winter cards (already clipped, if you did Part 1). Shuffle the remaining 180 cards. Split into 3 roughly even piles. Shuffle 2 Winter cards into one pile and cap that pile with the 3rd. Stack all three piles together and place the deck beside the Clearing.

Then together — about 30 seconds

Everyone grabs a Cave card (step 2) and draws their 6-card hand (step 7) at the same time. Start player is whoever most recently walked in a forest (step 8) — or just agree amongst yourselves.

Fast teardown

Two minutes now buys the two-minute setup next time. Before you box the deck, pull the 3 Winter cards back out and clip them together on top of the insert. Cave cards go back as a single loose pile — they're identical, there's nothing to reassemble.

How to play — teach script

Teach after setup, with the board and everyone's Cave card visible. The bold lines are written to be read aloud or paraphrased; the italics underneath are notes for you, the teacher.

0 · Buy-in (30 sec)

"This is a chill, ecology-building card game. You're each growing your own forest, one tree at a time, racing to build the most beautiful, best-scoring habitat before winter arrives. There's no combat and almost no way to hurt each other directly — the tension is timing: grabbing the right card before someone else does, and not getting caught out when winter shows up sooner than you'd like."

Sets expectations low-conflict, forgiving and family-friendly — good news for a first game.

1 · How you win

"Most points when the game ends wins. Every card in your forest — every tree, and every animal, plant or mushroom attached to it — scores points by its own rule, printed right on the card."

Don't detail scoring maths yet. Just: everything scores differently, and it's on the card.

2 · The round structure

"There aren't really 'rounds' — just turns, going clockwise. On your turn you do exactly one thing: draw two cards, or play a card."

3 · Your turn — the action economy

"On your turn, choose ONE: draw two cards, or play one card."

  • Draw two — each card comes from the top of the deck or from the face-up cards in the Clearing. Mix and match.
  • Play one card — pay its cost by discarding that many other cards from your hand into the Clearing, place the card in your forest, then use its effect and bonus if it has one.

"There are more effects and bonuses printed on individual cards — you'll read those as they come up."

Two clean options are all a new player needs to hold before playing.

4 · The core loop — teach this one properly

"Trees are the skeleton of your forest — each one has four slots: top, bottom, left, right. Every other card is a split card — an animal, plant or mushroom — and it has to slide into an open slot on one of your trees. Whenever you play a tree, you also flip the top card of the deck face-up into the Clearing — so playing trees feeds the shared pool everyone's drawing from."

"So: the Clearing is a shared buffet you're both feeding and eating from. What you discard to pay for a card might be exactly what an opponent wants next turn — and what you leave behind when you play a tree is a free gift to the table."

This is the one system that makes every play matter — what you pay with and what you expose is public and grabbable by anyone.

5 · What hurts you

"There's no punishment track here. The only real risk is being caught mid-plan when the game suddenly ends — winter doesn't wait for you to finish your turn."

6 · The clock and the exits

"Buried in the bottom third of the deck are 3 Winter cards. Draw one and nothing dramatic happens — it sits face-up next to the Clearing, and you draw a replacement. But the instant the third one is revealed, the game ends immediately. Right then — you don't get to finish your turn."

7 · "The game changes now" moments

  • 1st Winter card revealed: nothing changes yet, but the clock has started.
  • 2nd Winter card revealed: the game can now end on literally any draw. Start finishing plans instead of starting new ones.
  • Clearing hits 10+ cards at the end of a turn: it's completely wiped back into the box. Anything sitting there you wanted is gone — don't let a juicy discard sit too long before grabbing it.
Not applicable here

No predecessor. Forest Shuffle has expansions (Dartmoor, Exploration) but none of their rules apply unless you've added them to the box. No hidden layer either — no secret objectives, no hidden hands, no traitors. Every forest is face-up the entire game.

First-game recommendations

  • Mantra 1: "What you discard, someone else can take." Think about what you're leaving in the Clearing.
  • Mantra 2: "Trees first, matches second" — you can't place an animal or plant without an open slot to put it in.
  • Let the components teach. Read a card's scoring condition off the reference cards when it actually comes up, not before.
  • Appoint a Clearing-checker. Someone owns the "10+ cards → empty it" step. It's the one thing everyone forgets mid-turn.
  • The real teaching moment is the first time someone plays a tree and has to flip a card into the Clearing. Slow-walk that one together.

Defer these — do not front-load

  • Bonus payment-colour matching
  • Mushrooms' permanent effects
  • Per-species scoring maths
  • The Cave card mechanic
  • Tree Saplings
  • Reading the reference cards or digital appendix

Each item is one sentence when it's relevant on the table, and a paragraph of confusion if you explain it now.

Quick reference

Tap each time a Winter card is revealed at the table. Resets when you reload.

❄ Third Winter card revealed — the game has ended. No one finishes their turn.

The turn, in four lines

  1. Choose ONE: draw 2 cards (deck and/or Clearing) — or — play 1 card (pay from hand → place in forest → effect → bonus).
  2. Played a tree? Flip the top deck card face-up into the Clearing.
  3. Clearing at 10+ cards? Empty it into the box.
  4. Pass clockwise. The game ends the instant the 3rd Winter card is revealed.

The handful that break games

  1. The game ends the instant the 3rd Winter card appears — mid-turn if necessary. Nobody finishes what they were doing.
  2. Card removal before shuffling is based on player count — 30/20/10/0 cards go back in the box unseen for 2/3/4/5 players.
  3. The "redraw your hand if you have no trees" mulligan is not an official rule. Lookout removed it; some older summaries still list it.
  4. Playing a tree always flips a card into the Clearing — every single time, no exceptions.
  5. The Clearing empties completely once it hits 10+ cards at the end of a turn. Those cards are gone for the game.
  6. Hidden halves of split cards never count for anything — not for scoring, not for effects, ever.

Scoring checklist

  • Every visible card in your forest, by its own printed condition.
  • +1 point per card in your Cave.
  • Saplings score zero, but still count as trees for cards that count trees.
  • Mushrooms score zero — they're effects only.
  • A tie is a shared victory. No tiebreaker.
