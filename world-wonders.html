World Wonders

Raise an ancient city tile by tile, spending a fixed handful of gold each round. A tight, gold-starved builder where the resource you neglect is the one that scores you.

  • 1–5 players
  • 60–90 min
  • Arcane Wonders
  • Guides written for 5 players
Name check

This is Arcane Wonders' World Wonders, a city-building game about buying tiles and monuments onto a personal map. It is not related to, and shares no rules with, 7 Wonders. Don't let anyone import rules from memory of a similarly named game.

Fast setup

Roughly 12–15 minutes cold, 6–8 minutes once your group has bagged components per Part 1 and run it twice. Step letters (A–L) match the "Game Setup" section of the official English rulebook.

Part 1 — the one-time bagging pass

The goal: setup becomes grab bag → dump → shuffle, never sort. A dozen sandwich bags cover it.

Part 2 — parallel setup

One lane per player. Lanes 1–4 have no dependency on each other and run fully in parallel.

Lane 1Main board & tiles

Longest lane — give it your fastest sorter

Steps A, B. Place the Main Board plus the 4th and 5th Player Expansion Boards (both needed at 5 players) with their Building Tiles. Shuffle each shape-stack, place it face down on its marked spot, and reveal the top tile of each stack below it.

Lane 2Turn order

Steps C, D. Place the 1st and 2nd Player Turn Order Pedestals on the Main Board. Everyone draws a Player Marker at random and lines up on the Turn Order track in that order.

Lane 3Roads & Tower

Steps E, F. Place Road Tiles below the revealed Building Tiles per the 5-player count — 3 Long Roads, 2 Short Road sets. Place 1 Tower alongside the starting roads.

Lane 4Monuments & Loans

Steps G, H. Shuffle the Monument deck, reveal 3 cards, and place their matching wooden Monuments on top of each. Set the Loan Tiles face up in a pile beside the Main Board.

Lane 5Player kits

Everyone, simultaneously

Steps I–L. Grab your pre-bagged kit. Place your Map Board (matching everyone else's side), your Resource Board (matching side), and set Gold, Population and 3 Resource markers to 0. Keep your free Long Road tile in hand — it gets placed on your first turn, at no cost.

The one genuinely sequential step

Drawing Player Markers for initial turn order (step D). Do that together once Lane 2's pedestals are placed, since everyone needs to see the draw.

Then together — about 90 seconds

Confirm everyone agrees on Map Board side and Resource Board side. Confirm the starting turn-order line-up. Remind everyone to place their free Long Road tile on turn one.

Fast teardown

Monument, Public Objective and Solo Play decks each go back into their own band. Loan Tiles and Towers return to their central pools. Building Tiles get re-sorted by shape back into their permanent stacks — don't let this one slide; re-sorting 80 mixed tiles by hand is the slowest recovery job in the game. Each player's kit — Map Board, Resource Board (same side!), Player Marker, and 5 markers — goes back into that colour's bag together.

How to play — teach script

Teach after setup, with everyone's Map Board and Resource Board visible. Bold lines are for reading aloud; italics are notes to you.

0 · Buy-in (30 sec)

"This is a tight, ancient-world city-building game. You're each raising your own city from scratch, spending gold every round to buy roads, buildings, and eventually monuments onto your personal map. Expect a slow burn — early rounds feel gold-starved, and the game rewards patience and balance over rushing any one thing."

Sets the expectation that early rounds feel constrained by design. New players often think they're doing it wrong when gold runs out fast. It's normal.

1 · How you win

"Most Victory Points when the game ends wins. Points come from five different places — how big your population got, your worst-performing resource, your monuments, uncovered natural resources on your map, and fully-surrounded City Districts. We'll cover what each of those means as they come up — for now, just know points come from several directions, not one."

Don't detail the five categories yet. Just that they exist, and that balance matters more than any single one.

2 · The round structure

"The game runs in rounds — up to 10 of them. Each round, everyone gets 7 Gold. Going around the table, each player spends their gold one action at a time, buying things for their city, until they're out. Once everyone's out of gold, the round resets: new tiles come up, everyone's Gold refills to 7, and turn order gets rearranged."

3 · Your turn — the action economy

"On your turn you must buy exactly one item, spending Gold from your 7 (or up to 9 if you've taken a loan). The twist: nobody's out of the round until they've spent it all — so someone with 1 Gold left keeps taking cheap turns long after someone who blew everything on a Monument sits out."

The five things you can buy with your action:

  • A Road tile (1 Gold) — extends your street network.
  • A Building Tile (cost printed on the stack) — advances your resource tracks.
  • The Tower (2 Gold) — one available per round.
  • A Turn Order Pedestal (1 Gold) — locks in 1st or 2nd position for next round.
  • A Monument (all your remaining Gold) — big scoring pieces, and buying one ends your round on the spot.

"You can also take out or pay off a Loan on your turn — that's a free action alongside your required buy, and we'll cover it when it's relevant."

Enumerating every placement rule up front guarantees none stick. Five purchase options is learnable; adjacency rules get read off the board as they come up.

4 · The core loop — teach this one properly

"Every tile and monument you buy advances one or more of your three Resource tracks — Food, Ceramics and Gears. Whenever one of those tracks crosses a Population marker on its own scale, your Population advances too. More Population usually means more points — but here's the catch: at the end of the game, you only score points equal to your lowest resource track. Spend the whole game maxing out Gears while ignoring Food and Ceramics, and Food is what you get scored on."

"So: build wide, not tall. The resource you neglect is the resource that scores."

This is the rule that shapes every purchase decision. Everything else can be looked up mid-game; this one drives strategy from turn one.

5 · What hurts you

"The one real punishment: if you take out a Loan for extra Gold and haven't paid it back by the time the game ends, that's minus 2 Victory Points. It's a real option, not a trap — just don't forget it's there at the final scoring."

A delayed, easy-to-forget consequence. Flag it now so nobody feels blindsided at the scoresheet.

6 · The clock and the exits

"The game ends one of two ways: after 10 full rounds, when there are no more Building Tiles left to reveal — or immediately at the end of any round where someone's Population reaches the very last space on their track. Either way, once that trigger hits, everyone finishes the current round and then you flip to scoring."

7 · "The game changes now" moments

  • Someone buys a Turn Order Pedestal: they lock in 1st or 2nd position for next round regardless of Population — this can flip the normal "lowest Population goes first" catch-up order.
  • Someone buys a Monument: it costs everything they have left, and their round ends immediately.
  • A player runs out of Gold: they're done for the round but stay visible on the Turn Order track. New players sometimes think a player who's "out" has left the game state entirely; they haven't.
  • Someone reaches the last Population space: the game is now ending at the close of this round.
Not applicable here

No predecessor whose rules bleed in. No hidden information in a standard game either — every player's city, gold and resources are visible the whole time. If you're adding Public Objective Cards for Advanced Play, mention only that 3 get revealed and pay out bonus points at the end.

First-game recommendations

  • Mantra 1: "The resource you neglect is the resource that scores." Watch your lowest track, not your highest.
  • Mantra 2: "Being behind in Population buys you turn order." Lowest Population goes first next round — a built-in catch-up mechanic, not a reason to panic.
  • Let the components teach. Monument placement and adjacency rules are unique per Monument — read the card when you're actually buying one.
  • Appoint a round-reset owner. At the end of every round someone has to discard unbought tiles, reveal fresh ones, refill roads and the Tower, reset Gold, and re-sort the Turn Order track. It's the step every group forgets first.
  • The real teaching moment is the first round-reset — walk through all five sub-steps together, out loud.

Quick reference

Loan ledger

Tap a colour when that player takes a Loan; tap again as they repay. Unpaid Loans cost −2 VP each at final scoring — the delayed penalty everyone forgets.

The round, in five lines

  1. Everyone starts the round with 7 Gold (up to 9 with a Loan).
  2. In turn order, each player buys exactly one item — Road / Building / Tower / Pedestal / Monument — plus an optional free Loan action.
  3. Buying a Monument spends all remaining Gold and ends that player's round on the spot; otherwise a round ends once they've spent down to 0.
  4. Once everyone's round is over: discard unbought tiles, reveal new ones, refill Roads and the Tower per player count, reset Gold to 0, re-sort Turn Order by Population (lowest first, Pedestal-holders exempted).
  5. The game ends after round 10, or immediately at the end of the round in which any player's Population hits the final space.

The handful that break games

  1. Buying a Monument spends every last Gold you have and ends your round immediately, no matter how much you had left.
  2. You score points equal to your LOWEST resource track — Food, Ceramics or Gears, whichever is smallest. Not your total, not your highest.
  3. An unpaid Loan costs 2 Victory Points at game end, per Loan outstanding.
  4. Next round's turn order sorts by lowest Population first — a built-in catch-up mechanic, except for whoever bought a Turn Order Pedestal.
  5. The game can end early — the instant a player's Population marker reaches the final space, the current round finishes and then it's over.
  6. Natural Resources can be built over — but leaving them visible is what scores you points, not covering them.

Final scoring — five categories
